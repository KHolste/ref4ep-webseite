FORCETRACE

FORCETRACE computes what a divergent noble-gas ion beam in the keV range does to solid bodies and inside a vacuum facility: force and torque on a body at arbitrary inclination, heat load, sputtering and deposition, the expected reading of a thrust balance, the neutral gas density in the facility, and charge exchange and scattering of the beam up to the Faraday cups.

What is FORCETRACE for?

The program was created for laboratory experiments on the Ion Beam Shepherd principle: an ion beam is to push an object in orbit – such as a piece of space debris – without contact. The force transferred depends on the shape, attitude and material of the body, on the energy and divergence of the beam, and on which parts of the body shadow others. In the laboratory this force is measured with a thrust balance, whose reading additionally contains the torque, noise and thermal drift.

The second field of application is beam diagnostics in large vacuum facilities such as JUMBO at Justus Liebig University Giessen: how high is the pressure with the thruster running, what do the gauges read, and how much current do the Faraday cups lose to charge exchange and scattering in the residual gas? Other facilities can be imported as surface meshes.

Physical models and features

The collision physics at the surface comes from precomputed tables of the binary collision code RustBCA. FORCETRACE itself computes beam, geometry, shadowing, gas transport and charge exchange.

  • Momentum transfer from RustBCA – for every combination of ion, material, energy and angle of incidence, the momentum balance of backscattered ions and sputtered atoms, plus sputter yield and heat fraction. Values are interpolated between the tabulated points, but not extrapolated outside the computed range.
  • Beam model – source diameter, current, divergence and profile (Gaussian, uniform or measured from file); the distribution at the target is the convolution of the source profile and angular distribution, with curved trajectories for laboratory distances.
  • Force, torque, heat and erosion – balance at each sampling point of a triangle mesh; primitives (plate, cube, cylinder, cone, sphere, sheet) can be combined, and mass, centre of mass and inertia tensor are computed as well.
  • Shadowing – for non-convex and multi-part bodies, each sampling point is traced back along its beam path to the source. Without shadowing, the intercepted current for complex bodies would be overestimated by 20 to 30 %.
  • Deposition – flight of the sputtered material with the emission distributions from RustBCA, sticking probability and diffuse re-emission; the result is the film growth rate per surface.
  • Parameter studies – sweep distance, offset, angle of inclination, current, divergence or source diameter, with plots and CSV export.
  • Measurement planning on the thrust balance – simulated measurement cycle with control loop, noise, zero drift and thermal drift, analysed as in the laboratory; error budget by varying the uncertain input quantities, and search for the optimum cycle.
  • Ion Beam Shepherd in orbit – free motion of a body in the beam and orbit lowering over weeks, either orbit-averaged or step by step including Earth's oblateness, atmospheric drag and Earth's shadow.
  • Neutral gas in the facility – test-particle Monte Carlo in free molecular flow with thruster, turbopumps and cryosurfaces; optionally outgassing and leaks. Gas collisions can be enabled in the chamber simulation.
  • Gauges and residual gas – true pressure and the readings of cold-cathode and Bayard–Alpert gauges per gas; mass spectrum with isotopes, double ionisation and fragments.
  • Charge exchange, scattering and Faraday cups – charge exchange along the beam path from the computed gas density, elastic scattering in the ZBL potential and the geometric acceptance of the cup apertures; the result is the current per cup with and without these losses.
  • Custom facilities – import as a surface mesh (gmsh, STL) with a description of pumps, cryosurfaces, gauges and source; the enclosure is checked for gaps using test particles.

Ion Beam Shepherd

In the Ion Beam Shepherd concept, a shepherd satellite points its thruster at an object and pushes it with the ion beam; a second thruster compensates for the recoil. FORCETRACE computes how the object moves and rotates, whether the shepherd can keep its distance, and how long it takes to lower the orbit.

Verification

A built-in self-test checks more than 300 cases with known results. The collision physics itself is not tested here; it comes from RustBCA.

  • Closed-form solutions – force on a plate in the full beam to six digits, torque against a lever arm computed by hand, volume, centre of mass and inertia of the primitives.
  • Conserved quantities – the beam loses no current, the heat load stays below the incident power, and eroded and deposited material agree to within 0.01 % in a closed chamber.
  • Gas computation – in an example chamber with known pumping speed, the computed pressure lies within 3 % of the expected value Q/S; the charge-exchange fraction in homogeneous gas follows σ·n·L.
  • Comparison with SPARTA – cross-check of the gas computation with the DSMC code SPARTA on test geometries, including gas collisions.
