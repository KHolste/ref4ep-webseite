Why does the current of a hot cathode saturate?

A heated cathode releases electrons (thermionic emission). The maximum current density it can supply follows the Richardson–Dushman equation and rises steeply with temperature:

js = D T2 exp(−WA/kBT)

Without space charge the field between the plates would be uniform, the potential a straight line, and every electron would reach the anode: j = js, independent of the voltage (temperature-limited). But the electrons in the gap are charged themselves and screen the field.

Space charge

Poisson’s equation, energy conservation and charge conservation (j = ρv is the same everywhere) together give a differential equation for the potential:

d2φdx2 = −ρε0,   v = √(2eφ/m),   ρ = −jv
d2φdx2 = β φ−1/2,   β = jε0√(m/2e)

Child–Langmuir law

More current means more space charge and a weaker field at the cathode. The limit is reached at E(0) = 0. With φ(0) = 0 and φ(d) = U:

φ = U (x/d)4/3,   ρ ∝ x−2/3,   v ∝ x2/3
jCL = 4ε09√(2e/m) U3/2d2,   I = KU3/2

The perveance K = 4ε0A√(2e/m)/(9d2) contains only the geometry and the particle species. The diode is not ohmic: the power UI goes into the kinetic energy of the electrons and only becomes heat at the anode.

The virtual cathode

The electrons do not leave the cathode at rest but with thermal energy (a few tenths of an eV). If the cathode supplies more than the gap can take, a potential minimum φm < 0 forms in front of it. Only electrons with enough energy get across; the others turn back:

j = js exp(eφm/kBT)
n = nm eη(1 ∓ erf√η),   η = e(φ − φm)/kBT   (− anode side, + cathode side)

The minimum regulates the current by itself: more emission deepens it until just as many electrons get through as the gap can carry. The minimum acts like a cathode at xm where the field vanishes; the current then depends almost only on U and d. The app solves this model (Langmuir 1923) numerically; the cold Child–Langmuir law is its limit for eU ≫ kBT.

Two limits

Roughly the smaller of the two currents js(T) and jCL(U) flows. The characteristic I(U) first shows the slope 3/2, then saturation; I(T) first shows the steep Richardson rise, then a plateau that depends on the voltage. This is how Langmuir demonstrated space charge in 1913: his measured thermionic currents versus temperature show exactly these plateaus.

Electrons and ions

The same law limits the ion current extracted through the grids of an ion thruster. Because of √(e/m) it is about 490 times smaller for xenon with the same geometry. The maximum thrust density T/A = (8/9)ε0(U/d)2, on the other hand, does not depend on the mass; see the tutorial Ion extraction in a gridded ion thruster.
