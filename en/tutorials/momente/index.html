Moments of a distribution

Density, flow and temperature from the distribution function

The velocity distribution f(v) here is one-dimensional and made up of up to two Maxwellian components. The fluid quantities are integrals over f: density, mean velocity, temperature. The dashed curve is the Maxwellian with the same three values. That is all the fluid picture sees.

What the moments capture

Density, flow and temperature are the zeroth, first and second moments of f. The temperature is defined via the random motion w = v − u, even when f is not a Maxwellian.

What is lost

Very different distributions share the same n, u, T. The example “Same moments, different shape” shows two beams that the fluid picture cannot distinguish from a Maxwellian at rest. Only higher moments such as heat flux and kurtosis reveal the difference.

Units

Velocities in units of a reference velocity, temperatures as kBT/m in the same units squared, densities relative. The distribution is one-dimensional; in three dimensions the same definitions hold with vectors.
