Wave surfers

Landau damping in the picture of a surfer riding a wave

Electrons that move almost as fast as a wave behave like surfers: slightly slower ones are pushed along by the wave, slightly faster ones push the wave. Whether the wave loses or gains energy depends on which group is larger. First launch individual surfers, then predict in a guessing game what happens to the wave. The calculation with the Vlasov equation is shown in the tutorial Landau damping.

What does the surfer picture show?

In the co-moving frame the wave stands still and forms a landscape of hills and troughs of potential energy. A surfer slightly slower than the wave is pushed by the trailing slope and gains energy. A slightly faster one runs up against the leading slope, is slowed down and gives energy to the wave.

The wave loses energy if near vφ there are more slower than faster particles, i.e. if f′(vφ) < 0. This is the case for the Maxwellian. With a beam, f′(vφ) > 0 is possible; then the wave grows.

Why do the slow surfers gain on average?

Of the slightly slower surfers, at first one half is accelerated and the other decelerated. The accelerated ones approach the wave’s velocity, therefore stay longer in the pushing phase and keep gaining. The decelerated ones fall back faster and leave the braking phase sooner. On average a gain remains. For the slightly faster ones the same holds with the opposite sign.

In part 1, the curve ⟨ΔE⟩ shows exactly this average over a group.

When are surfers trapped?

A surfer stays trapped in a trough if |v − vφ| is smaller than the trapping width 2√A, where A is the depth of the trough (more precisely: its position in phase space must lie inside the dashed separatrix). Trapped surfers oscillate with the bounce frequency ωb = √A and on average move with the wave.

If the wave is small, the exchange ends before a surfer has completed one bounce: this is linear Landau damping. If the wave is large, the resonant surfers are trapped; the amplitude oscillates and settles at a residual level, and f(v) becomes flat at vφ.

What is computed?

In the wave frame, for each particle with position ξ (wave phase) and u = v − vφ: dξ/dt = u, du/dt = Im(b e−iξ). The potential energy is −Re(b e−iξ), its depth A = |b|. In part 1, b is fixed. In part 2 the wave changes due to the particles: db/dt = −i Σj mj eiξj (single-wave model). The combined energy and momentum of wave and particles are conserved.

Velocities are given in units of vth = √(2kBT/m), times in 1/(k vth) with the wavenumber k. 12,800 particles are computed in the window |u| < vth, weighted with f(v) ∝ exp(−v²/vth²). For small waves this gives γ ≈ (π/2) η f′(vφ) with the coupling η = 0.3.

How far does the picture go?

The surfer picture explains in which direction the energy flows. In a real plasma, however, the wave is itself an oscillation of all the electrons, and vφ follows from the dispersion relation. Here vφ and the coupling are chosen freely, and only particles near vφ are computed.

Where the information about the initial perturbation goes and why the damping remains reversible is not shown by the surfer picture. That is the role of phase mixing in the tutorial Landau damping.
