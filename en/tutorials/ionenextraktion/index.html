Where does the thrust come from?

In a gridded ion thruster, ions from the plasma are accelerated between the screen grid (x = 0, potential φ1) and the accelerator grid (x = d, φ2). The reaction force acts electrostatically on the surface charges of the grids. Model: parallel-plate capacitor; the grid apertures are neglected.

j = qn v = ρ v    current density
d2φdx2 = −ρε0,   E = −dφdx

Homogeneous space charge (simplest model): With constant ρ̄, φ becomes a parabola and E a straight line, U = φ1 − φ2:

E(x) = ρ̄ε0x + E0,   E0,d = Ud ∓ ρ̄d2ε0
ρ̄ = IA·1v̄,   v̄ = 12vex,   vex = √(2qU/m)

Force and thrust: With ρ = ε0 dE/dx, the force on all ions becomes an integral over E dE, regardless of the shape of ρ(x):

Fion = ∫0d ρE dx = ε0∫E dE = 12ε0(Ed2 − E02)
T = −Fion = 12ε0(E02 − Ed2)    per unit area

Each grid is pulled into the gap by the electrostatic pressure ½ε0E²: the screen grid forwards (+x), the accelerator grid backwards (−x). Because the ions strengthen the field at the accelerator grid (Ed > E0), this grid wins. Hence its name. The charge balance follows from Gauss's law:

σ1 = ε0E0,   σ2 = −ε0Ed,   σ1 + ∫ρ dx + σ2 = 0

Comparison with T = ṁvex: With ṁ = Im/q, T = I√(2mU/q). The homogeneous model gives T/A = −ρ̄U = −2jU/vex, which is the same. In general this is the momentum theorem: the force on the ions is their momentum flux, j/q · mvex.

Space charge and Child–Langmuir

Because of charge conservation, it is not ρ but j = ρv that is constant. Where the ions are still slow near the screen grid, they crowd together. Solved self-consistently, with c = √(2q/m):

ρ = jc√(φ1 − φ),   E2 = E02 + 4jε0c√(φ1 − φ)

More current lowers E0. At E0 = 0 the space-charge limit is reached; the grid cannot extract any more ions:

jCL = 4ε09√(2q/m) U3/2d2,   φ = φ1 − U(x/d)4/3
TmaxA = 89ε0U2d2    independent of the ion mass

The thrust density is therefore limited by the field strength: at 1000 V across 1 mm to less than 8 N/m². This is why ion thrusters have large grids with a narrow gap and still deliver only millinewtons of thrust.

More realistic: the beamlet (2D)

Real grids have apertures; each aperture forms its own partial beam, a beamlet. The 2D view computes one aperture axisymmetrically and self-consistently: Poisson equation with the ions from the trajectories and Boltzmann electrons in the plasma,

∇2φ = −ρi − e n0 exp((φ − φP)/Te)ε0

Where the potential falls below the plasma potential φP, the electrons vanish. This boundary is the plasma meniscus, the surface from which the ions emerge. Its shape depends on the current: with little plasma it curves back into the plasma (concave) and focuses strongly; the trajectories cross and then diverge. With too much plasma it becomes flat or bulges outwards; the beam becomes wide and hits the accelerator grid. In between lies the optimum perveance with minimum divergence:

P = IbUT3/2,   Pmax = 4ε09√(2q/m) πds24le2
le = √((lg + ts)2 + ds2/4),   UT = φ1 − φa

The accelerator grid is biased negatively. Downstream of the grid, the beam is kept electrically neutral by electrons from the neutraliser. Without the negative voltage, these electrons would be sucked back through the apertures into the thruster. The decisive quantity is the saddle point of the potential at the aperture centre: it must lie well below the beam potential (0 V). The thrust per aperture is smaller than ṁvex by the mean cosine of the divergence angle.

Tasks

    Model: one-dimensional parallel-plate capacitor; field distortions due to the grid apertures, beam spreading and collisions are neglected. The ions are singly charged and start from rest at the screen grid (x = 0); downstream of the accelerator grid the beam is fully neutralised (E = 0). The accelerator grid is at φ2 = 0, the screen grid at φ1 = U. Real accelerator grids are biased a few hundred volts negative so that no electrons flow back from the beam. The current density is averaged over the whole grid area, j = I/A; in the apertures it is actually higher, and there real grids operate close to the Child–Langmuir limit. Ion masses 132 u, 84 u and 40 u with 1 u = 1.66·10⁻²⁷ kg. “Homogeneous model”: ρ̄ = j/v̄ with v̄ = vex/2; the velocity follows from the energy, v(x) = √(2q(φ1 − φ)/m). “Self-consistent”: ρ = j/v(x), solved analytically, E0 by bisection. If the current exceeds the limit, jCL is used. NSTAR and NEXT: rounded indicative values for beam current, beam voltage and grid diameter; grid spacing estimated. The animation runs in extreme slow motion; the number of ions shown is proportional to the charge in the gap. The drift of the ions in the plasma towards the screen grid is only indicated (in reality they leave the plasma at roughly the Bohm velocity, a few km/s); the computation starts at x = 0. The neutraliser electrons are likewise only indicated. In the 1D view the x direction is to scale (grid thicknesses and grid spacing); the apertures are only indicated.
    2D beamlet: axisymmetric model of one aperture. The hexagonal cell of an aperture in the hole pattern is replaced by a circle of equal area, at whose edge the radial field vanishes (symmetry with the neighbouring apertures). Mesh 20 µm, Poisson equation with Newton-SOR, 150 ion trajectories, space charge iterated with under-relaxation until beam current and divergence no longer change. The ions start 1.2 mm upstream of the screen grid with energy Te (slightly above the Bohm criterion Te/2, which stabilises the computation); the plasma potential lies 3Te above the screen grid. Electrons in the plasma are Boltzmann-distributed; 0.5 mm downstream of the accelerator grid the ion space charge is taken to be fully neutralised, and at the end of the computational domain the beam potential is 0 V. If the Debye length is smaller than the mesh spacing, the sheath is only coarsely resolved. Aperture geometries of the examples: typical values, rounded from the literature for NSTAR, assumed for the RIT-2X.
