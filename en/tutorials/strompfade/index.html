Set-up

Thruster and neutraliser are mounted electrically isolated inside the chamber. All power supplies are referenced to a common rail, the SSG (secondary star ground), which plays the role of the spacecraft ground. The SSG can float freely relative to chamber ground; a voltmeter measures the voltage Vt = ΦSSG.

The PHV (positive high voltage, screen grid supply) puts the screen grid at ΦSSG + UPHV, the NHV (negative high voltage, accelerator grid supply) puts the accelerator grid at ΦSSG − UNHV. The ion beam leaves the system; the neutraliser emits electrons. Collector, front aperture and housing of the neutraliser are all at SSG potential, each connected via its own ammeter (Icoll, Iap, Ihous,NTR). The three leads join at one node; a further ammeter measures the total current INTR = Icoll + Iap + Ihous,NTR.

Sheaths

A sheath forms in front of every surface in the plasma. Electrons are faster than ions, so the plasma charges up positively relative to the surface until the currents balance. For a surface that collects no net current (floating), for xenon

Ush = Te ln√(mi/2πme) ≈ 5.3 Te   Te in eV, U in V
Ie = Ie,0 exp(−Ush/Te),   Ii = Ii,sat
The neutraliser

The RF plasma in the neutraliser emits electrons through the aperture. To remain neutral, it must give off the same charge as an ion current to the collector and front aperture. The electron emission is therefore limited by the ion saturation current Ii,sat, which increases with RF power:

IA = Ii,sat − Ie,0 e−Ush,NTR/Te,N ≤ Ii,sat
Iem = I0 exp(Uc/Tc),   Uc = ΦP,beam − ΦP,NTR coupling voltage
Current and voltage balance

Kirchhoff’s current law applies at the SSG node. If the SSG floats, no current flows to ground, IW = 0, and the system must emit as many electrons (net) as ions leave it:

INTR + Ithr + IW = 0   current law at the SSG rail
ΣISeg = Ignd = IW   ground rail and ground
−Ithr = INTR + ΣISeg   plasma: what the thruster emits goes to the neutraliser and the wall
INTR = Icoll + Iap + Ihous,NTR,   Ithr = INHV + Ihous,thr − IPHV
IW = 0  ⇒  INTR = −Ithr ≈ Ibeam   the PHV and NHV currents are measured on the SSG side of the supplies

Going once round the loop, ground → beam plasma → neutraliser plasma → collector (SSG) → voltmeter → ground, the voltages add up to zero (Kirchhoff’s voltage law):

Vt = ΦSSG = Ush,wall − Uc − Ush,NTR

If Vt is set more negative than the value the SSG would settle at by itself, the neutraliser has to supply more electrons than the beam needs. The excess goes to the chamber wall and returns through the voltage source: IW < 0. This is the chamber effect, which does not exist in orbit.

Wall probes and wall segments

The chamber wall is divided into ten mutually isolated segments: four each at the top and bottom, plus the downstream and upstream walls. Each segment is connected to the ground rail through its own ammeter. The sum of all segment currents is the current IW between chamber and SSG; the individual values show where ions and electrons arrive. Even at IW = 0, individual segments can carry current that flows through the ground rail to other segments.

Planar Langmuir probes in the wall measure the local current density at wall potential, or their floating potential. In the model, the ions come from the beam (downstream wall) and as CEX ions (side walls). How the electrons are distributed is an open question: anything from uniform (isothermal Boltzmann electrons, the same current density everywhere) to locally ambipolar (zero net current at every point). The slider “Electrons at the wall” mixes the two limiting cases.

je(s) = Ie,wall [ (1 − κ)/Awall + κ ji(s)/Ii,wall ]
Vf = Te ln(ji/je)   relative to the wall
CEX ions

When a fast beam ion meets a slow xenon atom, it can capture the atom’s electron (charge exchange). What remains is a fast neutral and a slow ion that follows the plasma’s electric field: to the wall or, if it is created close to the grid, back onto the accelerator grid. The neutral gas comes from the residual gas in the chamber and from the thruster’s un-ionised propellant, which is densest directly in front of the grid:

dICEX/dz = Ibeam σ(E) (nbg + nthr(z))
Model assumptions and sources

Model: a network model made of a few lumped elements, not a plasma simulation. The unknowns are the potential of the beam plasma ΦP,beam, that of the neutraliser plasma ΦP,NTR and, in floating operation, ΦSSG. The beam plasma stands for the entire plasma in front of the chamber wall; the beam ends on the grounded downstream wall of the chamber; the potential hill close to the grids (a few tens of volts higher there) and the decay towards the wall are not resolved. Electron current to the wall Ie = k(Ibeam + Ii,NTR)·exp(−ΦP,beam/Te,B), i.e. the plasma density in front of the wall grows with the beam current. Neutraliser: ion saturation current Ii,sat ∝ RF power, electron saturation current at the electrodes 196·Ii,sat (same area, √(mi/2πme) for xenon), 1 % of the ion current escapes through the aperture. The split of the collector current between collector (80 %) and front aperture (20 %) is assumed. Electron emission through the aperture Iem = I0 exp(Uc/Tc). CEX ions: Xe⁺ + Xe cross-section after Miller et al. (J. Appl. Phys. 91, 984 (2002)) at the ion energy e·UPHV; neutral gas from the residual gas (300 K, homogeneous) and the un-ionised thruster propellant (Q·71.8 mA/sccm − Ibeam, 400 K, free effusion from a disc with R = 4.5 cm, density on the axis); the neutraliser gas is not taken into account. Beam path to the downstream wall 4 m, beam attenuation included. Of the CEX ions created in the first 10 cm, half fly back to the thruster (70 % of these onto the accelerator grid), all others go to the wall. Potential in the beam: Boltzmann electrons with Te,B, beam radius R + z tan 15°, equal to ΦP,beam from zref = 1.5 m onwards; the CEX ion is created at rest and hits the wall with e·Φ(z); collisions on the way and the radial position in the beam are neglected, as is the back-reaction of the CEX ions on density and potential. Parameters: Te,N = 4 eV, Te,B = 6 eV, Ii,sat = 242 mA at 18 W, I0 = 33 mA, Tc = 8.8 V, k = 37. With these values the floating SSG settles at about −11 V for a beam current of 100 mA and at about −20 V for 200 mA. All potentials and currents shown are model values. Zener diodes (±50 V) between SSG and ground serve as overvoltage protection. All current paths to the SSG are fitted with ammeters; the PHV and NHV currents are measured on the SSG side of the supplies. The beam current is regulated as Ibeam = IPHV − INHV; the thruster thus emits IPHV of ions, of which the returning CEX ions INHV + Ihous come back. Wall: cylinder 2.6 m × 5.5 m, thruster 1.5 m in front of the upstream wall, not to scale in the drawing (near region stretched); ten segments, 38 wall probes in the section shown (correspondingly more around the full circumference). Beam ions Gaussian on the downstream wall (σ = half the beam radius at 4 m), CEX ions radially to the side wall (spread 0.25 m in z), ions from the neutraliser onto the top wall near the neutraliser; electrons according to the mixing approach with κ. The split of the housing current between neutraliser (30 %) and thruster (70 %) is assumed. Animation: every dot represents the same charge, so the number of dots launched is proportional to the respective current. Speeds: beam ions √(2eUPHV/m); electrons with their net drift, equal to the beam-ion speed (in the neutralised beam they move on average with the ions; their thermal speed at Te,N is about 35 times higher, but random, and only indicated as jitter); CEX ions √(2eΦP,beam/m); ions from the neutraliser √(2eΦP,NTR/m). “Compressed” shows them in the ratio √(v/vbeam). Time runs greatly slowed down; the paths are schematic (near region stretched); electrons hit the wall according to the electron current density of the wall model.
