Tutorials

Interactive visualisations of the physics of electric space propulsion and its testing in the laboratory. Each visualisation runs directly in the browser, without installation: adjust parameters, watch what changes, and read in the explanations why.

Available tutorials

The models are deliberately kept simple so that the relationships remain visible. They are no substitute for simulation or measurement.

Electric propulsion

Current paths of an ion thruster in the laboratory

A gridded ion thruster with an RF neutraliser in a vacuum chamber: where do the beam, neutraliser and wall currents flow, and where are the potentials? Power supplies, the common reference bus, ammeters, sheaths and charge exchange are shown as a circuit diagram with moving charge carriers. A network model computes the potential of the reference bus, either floating or fixed, as well as the current to the chamber wall and the readings of wall probes. It also explains why the thruster behaves differently in the chamber than in orbit.

Plasma physics

Plasma drifts

Motion of individual electrons and ions in electric and magnetic fields, simulated in 3D: gyration, E×B drift, gravitational, gradient, curvature and polarisation drift, magnetic bottle and radiation belts in a dipole field. For each drift, the simulation shows the trajectory, the guiding centre and the comparison with the formula.

Plasma physics

Debye shielding

How a plasma shields a local perturbing charge: the electrons are attracted, oscillate at the plasma frequency and finally form a cloud beyond which the field decays within a few Debye lengths. One-dimensional two-fluid model of an argon plasma, compared with the Debye–Hückel solution.

Plasma physics

Liouville's theorem

A region in phase space, filled with possible states of a system: in free motion the rectangle becomes a parallelogram, in the harmonic oscillator it rotates, in the pendulum it is stretched into a long filament. Its area is conserved (Liouville's theorem), yet the density at a fixed point still changes. Time, dynamics and position of the region can be adjusted.

From particles to the distribution function

Many particles as points in phase space (x, v), counted in cells: this is how the distribution function f(x, v) arises. The number of particles and the cell size can be adjusted; you can see how noisy the counted distribution is and when it smears out narrow beams.

Moments of a distribution

Density, flow and temperature as integrals over the velocity distribution. The distribution can be composed of two Maxwellian components. One example shows two beams with the same moments as a Maxwellian at rest: the information missing from the fluid picture.

Plasma physics

Collision operators compared

An electron beam on a thermal background relaxes in the Krook model and with a Fokker–Planck collision operator. With a Coulomb-like collision frequency that decreases at high velocity, a fast beam survives for many collision times; in the Krook model it disappears with a single time constant.

Plasma physics

Landau damping

A density perturbation in a collisionless plasma according to the linearised Vlasov equation: damping by resonant particles, comparison with the dispersion relation, phase mixing in phase space and time reversal. With collisions the perturbation no longer returns; with a beam the wave grows.

Plasma physics

Wave riders

Landau damping pictured as surfers on a wave: launch surfers at a chosen velocity, watch particle trapping in phase space and, in a guessing game, predict whether a wave grows or shrinks. The computation uses the single-wave model, in which wave and particles exchange energy and momentum.

Diagnostics

Retarding potential analyser

How a retarding potential analyser (RPA) measures the energy distribution of the beam ions: ions fly through the grids and are repelled by the retarding field if their energy is insufficient. Differentiating the current–voltage characteristic yields the distribution, with the main beam and slow ions from charge exchange. Noise, step size, smoothing and acceptance angle show how accurate the analysis becomes.
